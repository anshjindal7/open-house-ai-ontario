Review drafts
Approved short answers become training data for the interpreter
What to check (open for the checklist)
  • Every claim is supported by the cited source. Click a source to read it.
  • It applies the law to these facts, and says which way they point, whichever side that is.
  • If a deciding fact is missing, it says so instead of guessing.
  • For real cases: does it agree with how the Board actually ruled? If not, edit or reject.
  • Plain language, 2 to 4 sentences, no invented numbers or deadlines.
  • Reject drafts that predict who would win: the model gets outcomes wrong most of the time. A good short answer says which rules apply to these facts; the real decisions shown after it speak to outcomes.
